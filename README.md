# 🚦 Road Accident Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

Road accidents cause thousands of injuries and deaths every year, and understanding when and where they happen can help improve road safety.

An interactive Excel dashboard that analyses road accident data to understand how many casualties occur, how severe they are, and which conditions light, road surface, road type, location, time are linked to accidents.

The final dashboard puts all of these into one view, so anyone can explore the data and filter it using slicers.

## 🎯 Project Objective

To clean the raw accident data and build a dashboard that answers questions such as:

- What is the total number of casualties, and how do they split by severity?
- How have accidents changed month by month?
- How do light conditions and location (urban vs rural) affect accidents?
- Which road surface conditions and road types are most dangerous?

## 🔍 6. Key Findings

Based on the current workbook analysis:

- **Total casualties:** 417,883.
- **Slight casualties:** 351,436, approximately 84.10% of the total.
- **Serious casualties:** 59,312, approximately 14.19% of the total.
- **Fatal casualties:** 7,135, approximately 1.71% of the total.
- **Vehicle category:** Cars account for 333,485 casualties, approximately 79.80% of the total.
- **Location:** Urban areas account for 255,864 casualties, compared with 162,019 in rural areas.
- **Road type:** Single carriageways account for 309,698 casualties.
- **Road surface:** The dry-road category accounts for 279,445 casualties.

## 💡 7. Recommendations

Based on the findings from the road accident casualty analysis, the following recommendations could support road safety planning and further investigation.

### 🛣️ 1. Prioritize High-Casualty Road Categories

Single carriageways account for a large proportion of recorded casualties. Road safety authorities could investigate these roads further to identify high-risk locations, traffic patterns, road design issues, and possible safety improvements.

### 🏙️ 2. Investigate Urban Road Safety

Urban areas account for a higher number of casualties than rural areas in this dataset. Authorities could examine pedestrian crossings, junction safety, speed management, and traffic flow in urban locations. Further analysis of traffic volume can needed to compare risk fairly between urban and rural areas.

### 🚗 3. Focus on Car-Related Casualties

Cars represent the largest share of recorded casualties by vehicle category. Further investigation should examine occupant safety, seat belt use, speeding, collision types, and other contributing factors before deciding on targeted interventions.

### 🌧️ 4. Improve Road Surface Safety

Dry road surfaces account for the highest number of casualties in the analysis. However, this does not mean dry roads are more dangerous, because dry conditions may occur much more frequently. Authorities should compare casualty numbers with traffic volume and time spent driving under different road conditions.

### ⚠️ 5. Strengthen Safety Measures for Serious and Fatal Casualties

Although slight casualties represent the largest category, serious and fatal casualties require particular attention because of their severity. Further analysis could identify the road locations, collision types, and circumstances associated with these outcomes.
